## Teaching Philosophy
Growing up in a family of educators, I developed a lifelong appreciation for mentoring and knowledge sharing. My teaching philosophy is built upon three core pillars:

1. **Critical Evaluation Over Memorization:** True mastery in engineering requires evaluating method efficiency and identifying trade-offs. I design assignments that prompt students to stress-test theoretical concepts against real-world edge cases. For instance, in my digital image processing course, I assigned an "invisible watermarking" project in the frequency domain without warning students of its real-world vulnerabilities—forcing them to independently discover noise limitations, evaluate robustness, and propose algorithmic fixes.
2. **Encouraging "Extrapolation" Over "Interpolation":** Standard "interpolation" (combining known algorithms for predictable setups) is insufficient for novel engineering problems. In an era where AI excels at data interpolation, human engineers must excel at *extrapolation*—thinking outside established templates to create novel solutions.
3. **Bridging Rigorous Theory with Modern Tools:** Having taught both deep control theory and modern computational toolkits, I emphasize connecting abstract mathematical proofs directly with practical, hands-on programming (e.g., Python/OpenCV, MATLAB/Simulink).
